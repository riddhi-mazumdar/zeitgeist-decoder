# Search intent taxonomy

This is the reference for Phase 4. Every keyword you generate must be classified by intent — because the same topic produces wildly different keywords depending on what the searcher actually wants, and the wrong intent classification leads to wasted media spend or content nobody reads.

## The four intents

The standard taxonomy (Jansen, Booth, Spink, 2008; refined by Google's quality rater guidelines).

### 1. Informational
The searcher wants to learn something. They are not yet ready to act. Volume tends to be high; commercial value per visitor tends to be low (but cumulative value can be significant for SEO and brand building).

Typical phrasings: "how to," "what is," "why does," "best way to," "guide to," "meaning of," questions in general.

Example signals these map to:
- "what is soft life" — definitional, early-stage cultural curiosity
- "how to layer skincare" — utility, broad audience
- "is matcha better than coffee" — comparative, evaluative

Content fit: explainers, guides, comparison articles, definitional pieces, video essays.

### 2. Navigational
The searcher knows exactly which brand, product, or destination they want. They are using search as a routing tool. Volume is concentrated on brand names. Commercial value is high but mostly captured by the brand being searched for.

Typical phrasings: brand name, brand name + product, brand name + location.

Example signals:
- "nykaa" — direct navigation to the brand site
- "fabindia delhi store" — navigation to a specific outlet
- "loreal serum reviews on nykaa" — partly navigational, partly informational

Content fit: brand SEO, paid brand defence, microsite for product launches.

### 3. Commercial investigation
The searcher is considering a purchase but hasn't decided. They are comparing, reading reviews, narrowing options. This is the most strategically valuable intent for most categories because the searcher is in-market but not yet committed.

Typical phrasings: "best," "top," "vs," "review of," "alternatives to," "is X worth it," "X vs Y."

Example signals:
- "best matcha brand in india" — pre-purchase evaluation
- "the ordinary niacinamide vs minimalist" — direct comparison
- "is fabindia worth the price" — evaluation

Content fit: comparison pages, review content, listicles ("best X for Y"), buyer's guides. This is where most performance marketing efficiency lives.

### 4. Transactional
The searcher is ready to buy or convert. The decision is made, they need the path to action.

Typical phrasings: "buy," "order," "price," "discount code," "near me," "delivery," "checkout."

Example signals:
- "buy matcha online india" — ready to purchase
- "fabindia kurta sale" — price-sensitive purchase intent
- "nykaa first order discount code" — final-step intent

Content fit: product pages, paid search, conversion-optimised landing pages.

## How to classify a keyword

Apply the question test:
- Could the searcher be satisfied by a Wikipedia article? → Informational.
- Are they searching for one specific brand/place? → Navigational.
- Are they comparing, evaluating, or seeking opinions? → Commercial investigation.
- Are they trying to complete a purchase or action? → Transactional.

Edge cases:
- Branded comparison ("nike vs adidas running shoes") is commercial investigation, not navigational, because the searcher is comparing.
- "X near me" is usually transactional in retail/services categories; can be navigational in some contexts.
- Ambiguous queries (e.g., "matcha") should be classified as the dominant intent for that exact query, not the average across variants. Use Google's results page as a tiebreaker — if the SERP is dominated by listicles, it's commercial. If by product pages, it's transactional.

## Why intent matters for cultural strategy

When a cultural signal is emerging, the search behaviour around it typically progresses through the intents in order:

1. **Pre-search phase** — the signal exists culturally but has no search keyword yet (e.g., a new aesthetic before it has a name)
2. **Informational phase** — the signal acquires a name; "what is X" searches spike
3. **Commercial phase** — searchers start comparing options within the new category
4. **Transactional phase** — buying behaviour is normalised; "buy X" searches dominate

A brand's optimal entry point depends on its appetite for risk:
- Enter at pre-search if you have product readiness and can name the category (highest reward, highest risk)
- Enter at informational if you want to be the educator/authority (medium risk, builds brand)
- Enter at commercial if you want efficient performance marketing (lower risk, harder to differentiate)
- Enter at transactional if you want immediate sales (lowest risk, highest cost)

In Phase 4 of the workflow, the keyword cluster you produce should ideally have keywords across all four intents, so the strategist can see where current demand is concentrated.

## Output format

When classifying keywords in Phase 4, use this format:

| Keyword | Intent | Audience signal | Estimated volume | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| what is soft life | informational | curious millennials, post-burnout cohort | 1,200/mo (India) | Definitional, very early funnel |
| soft life vs hustle culture | commercial investigation | considering a lifestyle shift | 480/mo (India) | Higher consideration intent |
| best slow fashion brands india | commercial investigation | in-market for purchase | 2,400/mo (India) | Strong commercial value |
| fabindia kurta buy online | transactional | ready to purchase | 8,100/mo (India) | High-intent, brand-locked |

Hand this table to Phase 5. The artifact (brief, deck, or sheet) will be structured around the intent funnel.
